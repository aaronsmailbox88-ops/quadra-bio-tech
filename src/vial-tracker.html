How to weigh (the method this tracker expects)
  1. The scale. A 0.001 g (1 mg) pocket scale with a draft shield and calibration weights is enough for water quantities of 0.05 mL and up. The one this tracker was built around: MAXUS 50 g × 0.001 g milligram scale with 2 × 20 g weights (Amazon.ca). Check it against the 20 g weight before each session; it reads to 1 mg but is only accurate to about ±3–5 mg, and the tracker shows that uncertainty on every dose.
  2. Dry vial, cap off. Flip the plastic cap off first — it weighs 100–200 mg. Record the weight. This is the baseline.
  3. Water by weight, not by syringe mark. Tare the empty syringe on the pan, fill it, weigh again: the gain in grams is the millilitres. After injecting, weigh the vial: the gain over the baseline is what actually went in. The two should agree; if they don't, liquid stayed in the syringe or leaked.
  4. Concentration = label mg ÷ measured mL. Use the measured volume, never the intended one.
  5. Each dose: weigh before you inject. Tare the empty syringe, draw, weigh: grams = mL, and the tracker turns it into mg at the vial's concentration and compares it with your planned dose. Weighing the vial before and after is the cross-check.
  6. Keep conditions identical — same pan, dry outside, cap state the same every time.

The tracker records what you measured. It does not set doses, and everything it shows assumes the vial contains what its label says, which only a lab test can confirm.
